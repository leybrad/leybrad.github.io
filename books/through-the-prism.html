Through the Prism

An ongoing conversation across nearly four decades.

What happens when we look at the familiar from an unfamiliar perspective?

Through the Prism brings together three volumes of stories, essays, poems, and philosophical reflections exploring the curious experience of being human.

Some of these writings began nearly forty years ago. Others emerged much later. Together, they reflect a continuing fascination with life, language, imagination, relationships, perception, and the possibilities of understanding.

They are not so much a collection of answers as an invitation to discover new questions.

All three volumes are currently available individually. A combined edition is planned for the future.

Why a Prism?

A prism does not create the colors that emerge from it. It allows us to see something that was already there.

Writing has often served a similar purpose in my life.

A seemingly ordinary experience, an unexpected encounter, a conversation, or even a rather absurd question can reveal possibilities that were not immediately apparent.

Sometimes a story allows us to see something that an explanation cannot. Sometimes humor reveals something that seriousness obscures. Sometimes an imaginary world helps us understand the world in which we actually live.

The writings in this collection approach life from many different perspectives. They do not necessarily arrive at the same conclusions, nor do they need to.

Part of the pleasure is in discovering how differently something can be seen.

A Conversation Across Time

When I first wrote many of these pieces, I rarely knew exactly what I was writing about. I usually sensed that something interesting was nearby, just beyond the edge of whatever I understood at the time.

Writing became my way of following it, one sentence at a time.

Looking back, I realize I wasn't simply collecting stories or ideas.

I was collecting questions.
Some of them took forty years to finish asking.

Returning to these writings many years later has been something like encountering a younger version of myself.

He was curious, imaginative, ambitious, sometimes wonderfully absurd, and often quite certain that the next idea might explain everything.

I have become rather fond of discovering that it won't.

Yet I do not necessarily wish to replace his questions with my answers. There are things that the younger writer noticed that the older writer might otherwise have forgotten to notice.

Both voices belong in this conversation.

From the Notebooks

Long before these writings became books, many of them existed as handwritten pages, typed manuscripts, unfinished stories, and ideas scattered throughout notebooks.

Writing was rarely simply a matter of recording what I already understood. More often, it was a means of discovering what I was thinking.

Revisiting those earlier writings has allowed me to continue explorations that began decades ago.

Some ideas have developed considerably. Some have taken unexpected directions. Others remain wonderfully unresolved.

The collection is therefore not simply a preservation of earlier writings. It is also a continuation of them.
