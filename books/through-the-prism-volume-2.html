The joy of experiencing.

What happens when the world does not behave quite as we expect?

Encounter explores the experiences, relationships, conversations, and unexpected discoveries that emerge when our understanding of life meets something beyond itself.

Through stories, essays, poems, and philosophical reflections, this volume considers what happens when different perspectives, expectations, and ways of living come into contact.

When Life Interrupts Our Expectations

We carry with us an understanding of how the world works, how people behave, and what particular experiences are supposed to mean.

Life does not always cooperate.

A conversation takes an unexpected direction. A stranger behaves in a way we cannot explain. Something familiar becomes unfamiliar when viewed from another person's perspective.

Sometimes we discover that what we thought we understood was only one possible interpretation of what was happening.

Encounters can be uncomfortable, humorous, beautiful, frustrating, or wonderfully surprising.

They remind us that the world is considerably larger than our understanding of it.

The writings in this volume explore some of the possibilities that emerge when we allow life to interrupt our expectations.

The Worlds We Bring Together

Every person arrives at an encounter carrying a lifetime of experiences, memories, assumptions, fears, hopes, and ways of understanding.

Much of that remains invisible to the people they encounter.

Two people may participate in the same conversation and experience something quite different.

They may use the same words without intending the same meanings. They may disagree without understanding what they are disagreeing about. They may change one another without either of them realizing it.

These differences are not merely obstacles to communication. They are also part of what makes human relationships interesting.

Understanding another person does not necessarily require us to become more alike.

Sometimes it begins with becoming interested in our differences.

What I Could Not Have Imagined

Throughout much of my life, I have been fascinated by ideas.

I have enjoyed following them, questioning them, developing them, and occasionally becoming rather attached to them.

But many of the most interesting discoveries in my life did not begin with an idea.

They began with a person, a place, a conversation, or an experience that I could not have anticipated.

Sometimes an encounter challenged something I believed. Sometimes it introduced me to something I had never considered. Sometimes it simply made me laugh.

Many of those experiences found their way into my writing, though not necessarily in recognizable forms.

Fiction allowed me to explore possibilities beyond what had actually happened. Essays allowed me to reconsider what I thought those experiences meant.

Writing became another encounter with experiences I had already lived.

Inside the Volume

Encounter brings together imaginative fiction, philosophical essays, poetry, and reflections on human experience.

Its explorations move between the familiar and the unfamiliar, between the individual and the world beyond the individual.

Some of the writings invite us to consider the perspectives of their characters. Others invite us to reconsider our own.

The reader, too, becomes a participant in these encounters, bringing experiences and interpretations that the writer could not possibly have anticipated.

Beyond the Familiar

Living in different parts of the world has given me opportunities to encounter ways of living and understanding that differed considerably from my own.

My years in Cameroon, Thailand, and Sudan introduced me to people, languages, customs, beliefs, and experiences that I could not have fully appreciated from a distance.

Sometimes the differences were immediately apparent.

At other times, what surprised me most was discovering how much we had in common.

These experiences form part of the broader conversation behind my writing, teaching, photography, and artwork.

There is a difference between knowing something about the world and allowing ourselves to encounter it.

Encountering a Story Through Images

A reader encounters a story through words, imagination, memories, and personal experiences.

An illustration introduces another perspective into that encounter.

It may emphasize a relationship, capture a particular atmosphere, or draw attention to a moment that might otherwise pass unnoticed.

It need not determine how the story should be understood.

Instead, it can become another opportunity to experience the story differently.

When a Story Meets a Reader

A writer can create characters, describe experiences, develop ideas, and arrange words on a page.

But a writer cannot entirely determine what happens when someone else reads those words.

Each reader brings a different collection of experiences, associations, questions, and memories.

Something that seems significant to one reader may seem quite ordinary to another.

A reader may even discover something in a story that the writer had not consciously intended.

I find that possibility rather delightful.

Reading is itself an encounter, and the conversation need not end with the final sentence.
