Philosophical Reflection

A Thing Like No Other Thing

By Bradley Arnold

It was a thing.

A thing like no other thing.

Nothing like it had ever existed before, which made it difficult to say whether it existed at all.

It could do anything.

It could be everything.

And that included nothing.

In fact, it did nothing particularly well.

It did nothing nowhere at no time with no witnesses to say anything about it. Its nothingness was so complete that when viewed from a distance, it appeared to be a great big nothing of value, nothing to see, nothing to learn from.

Which is what made it remarkable.

Some said this thing was God.

Others said it was a dimension, or a field, or a misunderstanding of language.

Some said it was just our egos looking for something to kneel before.

Scientists called it infinity – outer space folded into inner space.

Monks called it emptiness.

Mathematicians circled it carefully and never touched the center.

They all agreed on one thing: 

everything pointed back to nothing.

They looked at the smallest things and the largest things and found the same result. Matter was mostly empty space. Time bent. Meaning dissolved. Everything that seemed solid turned out to be temporary.

And still – here we were.

The prophets said nothing was all, and all was nothing.

That nowhere was everywhere.

That time lasted no time at all.

They understood this perfectly well, but could not explain it.

That made nothing into something. Thus, it wasn’t nothing no more.

Because nothing cannot be explained.

It can only be noticed.

Words fail. Pictures fail. Proofs fail.

Even understanding fails.

Without nothing, there could be no something.

Without silence, no sound.

Without loss, no meaning.

People worry about nothing.They shouldn’t.

Nothing is free.

Nothing lasts forever.

And even if someday nothing becomes something, there will still be nothing everywhere, holding it all in place.

I write all this and think: this is nonsense.

But nonsense has no value.

And no value is worth nothing.

Which, of course, is the point.

I feel like I understand the universe.

I feel like I understand nothing.

And somehow, both feel true.
